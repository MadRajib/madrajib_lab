My very first contribution to Zephyr RTOS

by MadRajib Lab · March 09, 2026 ·
C Zephyr

drivers: wifi: esp32: retrieve scan results iteratively was my very first contribution to Zephyr project. In this post I want talk about the how I ended up submitting a PR to ESP32 wifi driver in Zephyr.

What all started it

It all started last year when I was looking for a job change in a different city. A company A's HR, which has one of it offices located in the city I was looking for, reached out to me for an opportunity in city A and B. I was not at all interested in city A but was looking for city B.

Unfortunately I was rejected for the interview for the city B since I didn't have any WLAN domain expertise ( there was only wifi department present in city B ). Later multiple HRs from the same company called for city A but when I told them that I was looking only for city B, my resume was not selected.

I already had an job offer in the current city hence I was pushing forward for the city B.

After my multiple attempt with the HRs failed I wanted to study about WLAN.

Exploration

I jumped directly to linux kernel WLAN driver code to understand how it worked. After spending 1 hr I just gave up!.

I had some experience with the kernel driver model but looking into the code I had no clue on what was going on, it felt so confusing. Then I realized that I need to know the theory on how the wlan worked before diving into the code. I searched for documents related to this topics but I quickly lost interest, since it was getting so complicated and confusing.

I suddenly realized that there is Zephyr RTOS, which should have WLAN/Wifi driver and since it for micro-controllers the wifi stack should be small and a bit easier to understand. This was just a speculation I had.

Zephyr Journey

The Zephyr code structure looks quite similar to linux kernel so navigating was not that hard, and I quickly ended up in drivers/wifi folder. And to my surprise I saw eps32 and espat folders. Till that point I didn't know Zephyr supported espressif chips.

I then quickly jumped to the esp32 driver code. Going over the code I saw some familiar esp32 apis (I had a little bit of experience working with esp32 IDF)
api_1, api_2.

I felt little confident that I might be able to understand this driver.
